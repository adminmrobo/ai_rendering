Making a mug 3D: adding depth to silhouettes to reach inside the cup

Core question · Can we recover the inside of the cup, which silhouettes could never reveal, using depth maps that measure "how far is it from the camera"? Will it still be right when drawn from a new angle?

In 2-4, no matter how many silhouettes we gathered, the inside of the cup stayed at 0%. Silhouettes only tell us whether something is "covered." A depth map measures, for each pixel, the distance from the camera to the surface. That tells us the space between the camera and the surface is empty. If we measure the distance from above to the bottom inside the cup, we can erase the voxels in between and carve out the inside. The final mission of Part 2 is to recover the mug this way and score it by drawing it from a sealed angle that was not used for carving.

The analogy anchor is everyday life (measuring depth with a ruler, a glass table, testimony from many people). The mug, the voxel grid (40×40×40), and the silhouette camera (8 around the ring, 15° height) are the same as in 2-4. The depth maps are also 64×64, and each pixel stores the distance from the camera to the surface. Only voxels at least 0.07 (about one voxel) in front of a surface count as "empty."

Why this topic

1Depth sensors are already in your pocket

The face-recognition camera in a smartphone, lidar, and the motion-recognition camera in game consoles all capture depth maps. A method that merges live footage from inexpensive depth cameras into a 3D model of an entire room appeared in 2011[2].

2The power of knowing "empty"

When depth maps are merged into a voxel grid, the key is erasing the space between the sensor and the surface as empty[1]. Silhouettes carve only the outer edge (2-4, visual hull[3]), but depth also carves concave inner parts.

3Evaluate on new angles

For the recovered 3D to be useful, it has to be right when drawn from angles it has never seen. Research on drawing new viewpoints, such as NeRF, also scores results on photos that were not used for learning[4]. It's the same idea as the "sealed test photos" in 3-5 of Part 3.

Big picture: the path of Part 2 in one line

1Depth

One depth map: "it's empty up to here"

Model: voxels between the camera and the surface = empty → erasedEveryday: put a ruler inside the cup and measure to the bottom

In everyday terms

If you look at a cup only from the side, you can't tell whether it's full or empty. If you put a ruler in from the top and measure down to the bottom, you know that length is empty. But if you try to put the ruler in from the side, the cup wall blocks it, and you can't measure the inside.

In the model

A depth map stores, for each pixel, the distance d from the camera to the surface. Among the voxels projected onto that pixel, the ones closer to the camera than d are empty (free-space carving[1]). We erase more of these from the visual hull carved from 8 silhouettes (2-4). Depth also measures only what is visible, so to carve the inside of the cup, you have to measure from an angle where the inside is visible.

The math: free-space carving

For depth camera c, let voxel center p project onto pixel πᶜ(p), let the depth at that pixel be Dᶜ, and let the camera position be oᶜ. If ‖p − oᶜ‖ < Dᶜ(πᶜ(p)) − ε, then p is empty (ε = 0.07, a margin about one voxel in size). Combined with the silhouette condition (outside means erased), the voxels that remain are {p : inside all silhouettes} − {p : in front of the surface in some depth map}. Curless and Levoy averaged the signed distance to the surface in each voxel to get a smooth surface[1]. Here we use only the simplest form (empty or not).

Where to measure: from above, and from under the glass table

Model: only the places the depth camera can see can be called "empty"Everyday: look up from under the glass table

In everyday terms

The undersides of objects on a table are never visible from above. A glass table lets you look up from below. Hidden places can be known only from positions where they are visible.

In the model

Put 4 and then 8 depth cameras above at 60°, and nearly all of the inside of the cup gets carved. But the wedge below the cup remains. The area right under the cup is blocked by the cup from every camera above. It disappears only after 8 more measurements from under the glass table (−30°). The remaining error splits into "where it could not be seen" and "how coarse the grid is."

The math: dividing the error

We split the difference between the carved solid H and the true shape X into H − X = (inside of cup ∩ H) ∪ (under wedge) ∪ (edge) and X − H = over-carved. The first three are places no camera could see, so they shrink only when we add cameras. The last is a matter of grid size and margin ε, so it shrinks when we make the grid finer or average distances as Curless and Levoy did[1].

3Scoring

Scoring on sealed new angles, and depth noise

Model: depth drawn from 6 new angles not used for learning vs. the true depthEveryday: believe the testimony only when several people agree

In everyday terms

If one witness wrongly says "that spot was empty," you reach a wrong conclusion. If you accept a claim only when two or more people say the same thing, one person's mistake has less effect.

In the model

We draw the carved mug from 6 sealed new angles that were not used for carving, and count the pixels whose depth matches the true depth within 0.1. Real depth sensors have depth noise. If one noisy reading says "empty," it punches through the wall. Erasing only when two or more maps agree that it is "empty" (voting) makes the result less sensitive to noise.

Walkthrough for beginners: what shows up on new angles

  1. Silhouettes are right, depth is wrong. Even the cups carved only from silhouettes (8 or 64 shots) match 93–95% of silhouettes from new angles. But the pixels matching depth are only 44.4% and 43.0%. The inside of the cup is filled in, so when you look at it obliquely from above, you see a "lid" instead of the bottom.
  2. Adding depth brings it to 86.7%. 16 shots from above and below give 86.7%, and 4 shots from above alone give 85.4%. Even the real cup drawn on the same grid scores 88.1%, which is the ceiling of this grid. Since all sealed angles are from above (20–75°), the wedge under the cup barely shows up in the score.
  3. Noise and voting. Up to noise 0.05, the margin (0.07) keeps the score from dropping much (84.9%). At noise 0.1, 1,005 wall voxels punch through and the score falls to 67.2%. Erasing only when two or more maps agree leaves 339 voxels punched through, at 78.9%. Without noise, voting carves less and costs a little (86.7% → 84.2%).

The math: voting and scoring

When each of k depth maps says "voxel p is empty" as e_k(p) ∈ {0, 1}, we erase p if Σₖ eₖ(p) ≥ v (v = 1 or 2). If, at noise σ, the probability that one map wrongly calls a wall voxel "empty" is q, then with v = 2 the probability of erasing it by mistake falls roughly on the order of q² (assuming the noise across photos is independent). Scoring is, for each sealed camera c, the fraction of pixels where |Ẑᶜ(pixel) − Zᶜ(pixel)| < 0.1. If the drawn side is empty, it counts as wrong.

Code 1 · Carving with depth: where and how many depth cameras do we need to carve the inside of the cup and the bottom?
# 코드 1 · 깊이로 깎기 — 실루엣 8장에 깊이 사진을 더하면 컵 속과 컵 밑이 얼마나 깎이나
import numpy as np
def cyl(p, r, h):
    dx = np.hypot(p[..., 0], p[..., 2]) - r; dy = np.abs(p[..., 1]) - h/2
    return np.minimum(np.maximum(dx, dy), 0) + np.hypot(np.maximum(dx, 0), np.maximum(dy, 0))
def mug(p, handle=True):
    """위가 열린 머그컵(반지름 1, 높이 1.6, 벽 0.14) + 오른쪽(+x) 손잡이 고리"""
    body = np.maximum(cyl(p, 1, 1.6), -cyl(p - [0, .14, 0], .86, 1.6))
    q = np.hypot(p[..., 0] - 1.05, p[..., 1]) - .42; return np.minimum(body, np.hypot(q, p[..., 2]) - .09) if handle else body
def camera(az, el, dist=4.5, f=2.2):
    a, e = np.radians(az), np.radians(el); pos = dist*np.array([np.sin(a)*np.cos(e), np.sin(e), np.cos(a)*np.cos(e)])
    fw = -pos/np.linalg.norm(pos); rt = np.array([fw[2], 0, -fw[0]]); rt /= np.linalg.norm(rt); return pos, fw, rt, np.cross(rt, fw), f
def depth(cam, res=64):
    """광선 따라 걷기로 픽셀마다 카메라에서 표면까지의 거리. 안 맞으면 0 (실루엣 = 깊이 > 0)"""
    pos, fw, rt, up, f = cam
    u, v = np.meshgrid((np.arange(res) + .5)/res*2 - 1, 1 - (np.arange(res) + .5)/res*2)
    d = fw*f + rt*u[..., None] + up*v[..., None]; d /= np.linalg.norm(d, axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    t = np.zeros(u.shape); hit = np.zeros(u.shape, bool)
    for _ in range(96):
        s = mug(pos + d*t[..., None]); hit |= s < 1e-3; t = np.where(hit, t, t + .9*s)
    return np.where(hit & (t < 40), t, 0.0)
g, B = 40, 1.7; c = -B + (np.arange(g) + .5)*2*B/g
P = np.stack(np.meshgrid(c, c, c, indexing='ij'), -1).reshape(-1, 3)        # 40×40×40 칸의 가운데 점
true = mug(P) < 0; inside = cyl(P, 1, 1.6) < 0                              # 진짜 컵, 속을 채운 컵
def carve(sil_cams, dep_cams=(), res=64, margin=.07, noise=0, vote=1, seed=7):
    """실루엣 사진: 밖이면 지움. 깊이 사진: 카메라와 표면 사이(margin 이상 앞)의 칸은 비었다 → vote장 이상이 그렇다고 하면 지움"""
    keep = np.ones(len(P), bool); empty = np.zeros(len(P), int); rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    for cam, use_depth in [(c, False) for c in sil_cams] + [(c, True) for c in dep_cams]:
        pos, fw, rt, up, f = cam; q = P - pos; z = q @ fw
        x = np.floor((f*(q @ rt)/z + 1)/2*res).astype(int); y = np.floor((1 - f*(q @ up)/z)/2*res).astype(int)
        ok = (x >= 0) & (y >= 0) & (x < res) & (y < res); D = depth(cam, res)
        if use_depth and noise: D = np.where(D > 0, D + rng.normal(0, noise, D.shape), 0)
        d = D[np.clip(y, 0, res - 1), np.clip(x, 0, res - 1)]
        keep &= ok & (d > 0)
        if use_depth: empty += ok & (d > 0) & (np.linalg.norm(q, axis=1) < d - margin)
    return keep & (empty < vote)
iou = lambda a, b: (a & b).sum()/(a | b).sum()
ring = lambda n, el=15: [camera(360*k/n, el) for k in range(n)]
cavity = inside & ~true
print(f"진짜 컵 {true.sum()}칸 · 컵 속 {cavity.sum()}칸 · 실루엣의 천장 {100*iou(true, true | inside):.1f}%")
for name, dep in [("깊이 없음 (실루엣 8장만)", []), ("앞에서 1장 (15°)", [camera(0, 15)]), ("비스듬히 위 1장 (60°)", [camera(0, 60)]),
                  ("바로 위 1장", [camera(0, 89)]), ("위 4장 (60°)", ring(4, 60)), ("위 8장 (60°)", ring(8, 60)), ("위 8장 + 아래 8장", ring(8, 60) + ring(8, -30))]:
    H = carve(ring(8), dep); below = (H & ~true & ~inside & (P[:, 1] < -.8)).sum()
    print(f"{name:16s}: 겹친 비율 {100*iou(true, H):5.1f}% · 컵 속 깎임 {100*(~H[cavity]).mean():5.1f}% · 컵 밑 쐐기 {below:4d}칸")

def mulberry32(a):
    """화면(JavaScript)과 같은 난수: 같은 시드면 같은 봉인 각도·같은 잡음이 나온다"""
    def r():
        nonlocal a
        a = (a + 0x6D2B79F5) & 0xFFFFFFFF
        t = ((a ^ (a >> 15)) * (1 | a)) & 0xFFFFFFFF
        t = (t + (((t ^ (t >> 7)) * (61 | t)) & 0xFFFFFFFF)) & 0xFFFFFFFF ^ t
        return ((t ^ (t >> 14)) & 0xFFFFFFFF)/4294967296
    return r
def carve(sil_cams, dep_cams=(), res=64, margin=.07, noise=0, vote=1, seed=7):
    """실루엣 사진: 밖이면 지움. 깊이 사진: 카메라와 표면 사이(margin 이상 앞)의 칸은 비었다 → vote장 이상이 그렇다고 하면 지움"""
    keep = np.ones(len(P), bool); empty = np.zeros(len(P), int); r = mulberry32(seed)
    for cam, use_depth in [(c, False) for c in sil_cams] + [(c, True) for c in dep_cams]:
        pos, fw, rt, up, f = cam; q = P - pos; z = q @ fw
        x = np.floor((f*(q @ rt)/z + 1)/2*res).astype(int); y = np.floor((1 - f*(q @ up)/z)/2*res).astype(int)
        ok = (x >= 0) & (y >= 0) & (x < res) & (y < res); D = depth(cam, res)
        if use_depth and noise:                                         # 화면과 같은 순서로 잡음(상자-뮬러): 맞은 픽셀마다 난수 2개
            D = D.copy(); flat = D.reshape(-1)
            for i in np.flatnonzero(flat > 0):
                u, v = r(), r(); flat[i] += noise*np.sqrt(-2*np.log(u + 1e-9))*np.cos(6.283*v)
        d = D[np.clip(y, 0, res - 1), np.clip(x, 0, res - 1)]
        keep &= ok & (d > 0)
        if use_depth: empty += ok & (d > 0) & (np.linalg.norm(q, axis=1) < d - margin)
    return keep & (empty < vote)
iou = lambda a, b: (a & b).sum()/(a | b).sum()
ring = lambda n, el=15: [camera(360*k/n, el) for k in range(n)]
def render_voxels(keep, cam, res=64):
    """깎은 칸들을 새 카메라로 그리기: 광선을 0.02씩 걸으며 처음 만난 칸까지의 거리"""
    pos, fw, rt, up, f = cam; V = keep.reshape(g, g, g); s = 2*B/g
    u, v = np.meshgrid((np.arange(res) + .5)/res*2 - 1, 1 - (np.arange(res) + .5)/res*2)
    d = fw*f + rt*u[..., None] + up*v[..., None]; d /= np.linalg.norm(d, axis=-1, keepdims=True); Z = np.zeros(u.shape)
    for t in np.arange(4.5 - 2.8, 4.5 + 2.8, .02):
        idx = np.floor((pos + d*t + B)/s).astype(int); inb = ((idx >= 0) & (idx < g)).all(-1); c = np.clip(idx, 0, g - 1)
        hit = inb & V[c[..., 0], c[..., 1], c[..., 2]] & (Z == 0); Z[hit] = t
    return Z
r31 = mulberry32(31); sealed = [camera(360*r31(), 20 + 55*r31()) for _ in range(6)]   # 채점에만 쓰는 각도 (화면과 같은 6개)
truthZ = [depth(c) for c in sealed]
def score(keep):
    good = n = 0
    for c, A in zip(sealed, truthZ):
        Bz = render_voxels(keep, c); m = A > 0; e = np.where(Bz > 0, np.abs(Bz - A), 1)
        good += (e[m] < .1).sum(); n += m.sum()
    return 100*good/n

        Example values and illustrative models

        • The mug, voxel grid (−1.7 to 1.7 in 40 cells), and silhouette camera (8 views around, height 15°, distance 4.5) are the same as in 2-4. The depth camera is also at distance 4.5, 64×64. "Below" means a height of −30°, assuming a view from under a glass table.
        • The depth maps are exact distances obtained by ray marching. The noise in ③ is Gaussian noise added to each depth value (seed 7). Margin ε = 0.07.
        • The 6 sealed new views were drawn from random numbers with seed 31, at 0–360° around and 20–75° in height. Scoring is the ratio of pixels where the rendered depth matches within 0.1 at pixels where the true cup is visible; if the rendered side is empty, it counts as wrong. The carved solid is drawn by stepping rays in increments of 0.02 and using the first cell hit.
        • "Wedge under the cup" means cells that are neither the true cup nor the cup interior, but remain below height −0.8 (the cup bottom). "Edges" are the other under-carved cells.
